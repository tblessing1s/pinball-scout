Beginner-first discovery

Not sure where to start? We’ll help you narrow down your first pin.

Instead of expecting you to already know machine names, this flow helps you narrow the field, learn from videos and real-world play, refine your shortlist, and then take the next step toward actually getting the machine you want.

What this does

  1. Collect a few practical constraints
  2. Show a focused set of beginner-friendly machines
  3. Learn from your reactions to images and videos
  4. Suggest nearby taste-probe games to try
  5. Give you a confident top-3 shortlist and sourcing handoff

How to choose

What first-time buyers should optimize for

Favor confidence over perfection

Your first machine does not need to be your forever machine. The goal is to get to a strong shortlist you can feel good about, not a mathematically perfect answer.

Be honest about access

If you cannot easily play many machines in person, safer first-buy candidates and stronger resale matter more than usual.

React to machines, not jargon

Most beginners get better answers by seeing machines and reacting to them than by guessing how much they care about abstract pinball terms.

Still learning the language?

Use the glossary only when you need it.

You do not need to know terms like flow or rules depth before you can narrow down a good first pin. But if they come up, the glossary keeps them simple.
